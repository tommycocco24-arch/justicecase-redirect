About Justice Case

How the briefs are made

Each case's opinion comes from CourtListener, the Free Law Project's free database of court opinions; if it isn't available there, from Harvard Law School Library's Caselaw Access Project (case.law) or the Library of Congress's official U.S. Reports. Each brief page says which. An AI model writes the brief using only that opinion. A second step lists every assertion in the brief, and two different AI models from different companies (Claude, and an OpenAI or Google Gemini model) each check every assertion against the opinion, quoting the passage they relied on. Every quote is then checked word for word against the opinion text. Each brief page shows the results, and you can click any quote to see it in the opinion.

The brief format and study approach are adapted from Anthropic's open-source law-student plugin (Apache License 2.0).

Use it to learn, not instead of learning

A brief you didn't write is a brief you won't remember. Try Write it yourself first on any case page, or turn on Cold-call quiz to hide the answers. Before using any AI tool for graded work, check your school's honor code and your professor's syllabus.

Not legal advice

This is a study aid. Briefs can be wrong even after checking, and later history (appeals, cases that limit or overrule a decision) isn't checked. Nothing here is legal advice. If you're dealing with a real legal problem, contact a lawyer, legal aid, or your school's clinic.

Sources and rights

U.S. court opinions are public records and not subject to copyright. Opinion text is from CourtListener (Free Law Project), the Caselaw Access Project (Harvard Law School Library), or the U.S. Reports at the Library of Congress; only the courts' opinions are used, never reporter headnotes. The briefs are original summaries; no casebook or commercial headnote content is used.

Privacy

This site has no ads, analytics, or trackers. It's hosted on GitHub Pages, which, like any web host, may log visitors' IP addresses for security (see GitHub's privacy statement). Reading briefs never needs an account.

Without an account, the cases you save, your notes, and your practice answers stay in your browser and are never sent anywhere.

Justice Case Live, the optional tool for briefing other cases, runs on claude.ai under your own Claude account and Anthropic's privacy policy.
