Martin v. State

Black-Letter Rule

Alabama's public-drunkenness statute presupposes a voluntary appearance in the public place. A charge of drunkenness in a designated public place cannot be proved by showing that the accused, while intoxicated, was involuntarily and forcibly carried to that place by the arresting officer.

Facts

  • Charge: Appellant was convicted of being drunk on a public highway.
  • Arrest: Officers arrested him at his home.
  • Movement to the highway: The officers took him from his home onto the highway.
  • Conduct on the highway: Once there, he allegedly manifested a drunken condition by using loud and profane language.
  • Statute: Code 1940, Title 14, Section 120 punishes "[a]ny person who, while intoxicated or drunk, appears in any public place where one or more persons are present, * and manifests a drunken condition by boisterous or indecent conduct, or loud and profane discourse."

Procedural Posture

The trial court convicted appellant, and he appealed. The Court of Appeals first affirmed, then withdrew that original opinion of affirmance and issued this opinion.

Issue

Can a defendant be convicted of public drunkenness under Code 1940, Title 14, Section 120 when he was in the public place only because officers arrested him at his home and took him there?

Holding

No. The statute presupposes a voluntary appearance, so drunkenness in a public place cannot be established by proof that officers involuntarily and forcibly carried the accused there; the conviction was reversed and judgment rendered discharging appellant (opinion by Simpson, J.; no vote stated).

Reasoning

  • Statutory text: The court read the statute's requirement that the accused "appears in any public place" to presuppose a voluntary appearance. It rested this on "the plain terms of this statute."
  • Elements under the statute, as quoted:
  • The accused is intoxicated or drunk;
  • The accused appears in a public place;
  • One or more persons are present there; and
  • The accused manifests a drunken condition by boisterous or indecent conduct, or by loud and profane discourse.

The court's gloss attaches a voluntariness requirement to element 2.

  • Persuasive authority: The court adopted a rule "declared" elsewhere, which it called "sound": an accusation of drunkenness in a designated public place cannot be established by proof that the accused was involuntarily and forcibly carried there by the arresting officer. It cited three Georgia cases (Thomas v. State, Reddick v. State, Gunn v. State) and 28 C.J.S., Drunkards, § 14.
  • Application: Appellant reached the highway only because officers arrested him at home and took him there. His conviction was "contrary to this announced principle."
  • Disposition: Because "no legal conviction can be sustained under the evidence," the court reversed and rendered judgment discharging appellant rather than remanding for a new trial. It cited Code 1940, Title 7, Section 260, Robison v. State, and Atkins v. State for that "prevailing rule."

Concurrences & Dissents

None in the opinion text.

Notes

  • Ground of decision: The opinion rests on statutory interpretation. It reads voluntariness into the "appears" element of this statute. It does not announce a general constitutional or common-law voluntary-act principle.
  • Effect of later conduct: The court did not treat appellant's loud and profane language on the highway as curing the involuntary appearance. Meeting the manifestation element did not satisfy the appearance element.
  • Elided statutory text: The opinion quotes the statute with an omission (" *"). Only the quoted portions are before the reader.
  • Not addressed: The opinion does not discuss intoxication at home, the lawfulness of the arrest, or any facts beyond those stated.
  • Relied on (substantive rule): Thomas v. State, 33 Ga. 134, 125 S.E. 778; Reddick v. State, 35 Ga. 256, 132 S.E. 645; Gunn v. State, 37 Ga. 333, 140 S.E. 524; 28 C.J.S., Drunkards, § 14, p. 560.
  • Relied on (reverse and render): Code 1940, Title 7, Section 260; Robison v. State, 30 Ala.App. 12, 200 So. 626; Atkins v. State, 27 Ala.App. 212, 169 So. 330.
  • Own prior opinion withdrawn: The court withdrew its original opinion of affirmance as "laid in error." No other case is overruled or distinguished.
